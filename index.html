What I've been working on

STRATHcube

Student satellite team, University of Strathclyde · 2024 to now

STRATHcube is a CubeSat designed and built by students at Strathclyde, and it's going through ESA's Fly Your Satellite! programme. I joined in my first year, co-led the AIV team through our Final Design Review, and I'm now leading AIV for 2026/27.

In April 2026 we took the design to ESA's ESTEC centre in the Netherlands for the Final Design Review. I presented to a panel of ESA engineers, and worked through the Review Item Discrepancies (RIDs) they raised with the team during the colocation week.

Most of my AIV work is making sure every requirement on the spacecraft has a way of being proven. I compiled our Technical Specification Verification Control Document (TS-VCD) to ECSS standards, which tracks each requirement and whether it's closed by test, analysis, design review or inspection.

A cleanroom for building the satellite

STRATHcube · 2026

You can't assemble flight hardware on an open bench, so STRATHcube needs its own cleanroom before the 2027 build. I've been working with external suppliers to design and source a 12 m² ISO 7/8 cleanroom for assembly and testing.

It started with a measured sketch of the room: walls, radiators, windows, power and fire exits. The enclosure runs along the long wall between the two radiators, next to an existing power strip, which keeps the windows, sink and both fire exits clear. The supplier turned that layout into the design below, with an anteroom at the entrance. Procurement is under way.

Portable speaker

Advanced Higher Graphic Communication · Design project

For my Advanced Higher Graphic Communication project I designed a portable speaker from scratch: a cylindrical ABS body with passive radiators at each end and an aluminium carry handle. The project covered the full design package: a 3D model in Fusion 360, dimensioned engineering drawings, photorealistic renders, and marketing graphics for an imagined brand, SPKR.

To check the design would hold up in use, I ran static stress studies in Fusion 360 on the three parts most likely to fail. For each one I set the right material, fixed the model with constraints, and applied a load that matched real use.

None of the parts came close to failing. Fusion flagged the handle and housing as over-engineered, so the next design iteration would take material out of them. The housing did show a small peak in stress directly under the load, which is the spot I'd watch if the ABS got warm.

Solar array HDRM
